Hardness, in units you can use

Water reports list hardness in milligrams per liter (or parts per million) as calcium carbonate. Softener sizing and most homeowner advice use grains per gallon. The conversion is simple — one grain per gallon is about 17.1 mg/L — but nobody remembers it at the kitchen table. Use the converter, then read the scale beneath it.

Reading the City of Bonney Lake report

The report opens with the sources: Victor Falls and Grainger Springs, the City's wells, and the agreement with Tacoma Water that supplements supply. Groundwater that has filtered through the Plateau's glacial deposits picks up calcium and magnesium on the way, which is why Bonney Lake's supply reads moderately hard while Tacoma's Green River surface water reads much softer. If you live in Tehaleh or Prairie Ridge, note that your water may come partly or entirely from Tacoma through an intertie, and your hardness will be lower than the City's figure.

Then come the tables. Look for hardness (sometimes "total hardness as CaCO₃"), and if it isn't listed, for calcium and magnesium. Look at iron and manganese — small amounts explain orange and black staining and tell you whether a softener needs an iron filter ahead of it. Look at the chlorine residual, which is what you taste. And look at the lead and copper section: the City samples at customer taps, and the result reflects the private plumbing in older homes as much as the water itself. Everything else in the report is about safety, which the City handles; hardness, iron and chlorine are about your plumbing, which is where we come in.

What hardness does to a Bonney Lake house

  • Water heaters. Scale settles at the bottom of a tank, insulating the burner from the water, so the heater runs longer, rumbles, and fails early. In a tankless unit it narrows the heat exchanger. Annual flushing and descaling slow it; a softener stops it. See water heaters.
  • Fixtures. Aerators and shower heads clog, cartridges stick, toilet rim jets close up. The white crust on glass doors and the ring in the kettle are the visible version.
  • Appliances. Dishwasher elements, washing machine valves, coffee makers and the fridge's water valve all scale. Detergent works less well, so people use more.
  • Skin and hair. Not a plumbing issue, but the most common reason people call about a softener.

Softener, conditioner or filter?

A salt-based softener removes hardness by ion exchange and is the only thing that actually makes water soft. A salt-free conditioner changes the way scale forms so it doesn't stick as readily, without removing minerals — useful for people who don't want salt or the slick feel, with honest limits. A carbon filter removes chlorine taste and does nothing for hardness; an iron filter removes iron and does nothing for hardness either. Plenty of Plateau homes benefit from a softener plus a filter stage; the test at your tap decides which combination, and we'll tell you if the answer is none.

Three Bonney Lake households, three answers

A family in Tehaleh on Tacoma Water asked about a softener because the shower door spots. Their tap tested slightly hard; a softener would have been a waste. A squeegee and a carbon filter for chlorine taste were the honest recommendation. A couple on the original Bonney Lake lots had replaced two water heaters in twelve years. Their City water tested at the top of the moderately hard range; a softener and a new heater with an expansion tank ended the cycle. A homeowner outside Buckley on a well wanted a softener for the orange staining. The lab panel showed iron well above what a softener can handle; an air-injection iron filter first, then a softener, was the system that worked. The report gives you the range. The tap gives you the answer.

Maintenance on hard water

Whether or not you soften, two habits protect the house. Flush the water heater every year — a hose on the drain valve, ten minutes, done — and check the anode rod every two or three. On a tankless unit, descale annually through the isolation valves. Clean aerators and shower heads with a vinegar soak when flow drops. And if you do install a softener, set it to your measured hardness rather than the factory default; an over-set softener wastes salt and water, an under-set one leaves scale.

Testing: report vs. tap

The City's report is an average across the system and the year. Your tap is specific.
