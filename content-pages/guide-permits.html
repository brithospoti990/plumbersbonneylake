Where does my water heater permit go?

What the inspector actually checks

A water heater inspection is short, and almost every item on the list is something that matters more than the tank itself:

  • Seismic strapping — two straps, upper and lower third, anchored to framing. Western Washington requires it.
  • Temperature-and-pressure relief valve with a discharge line of the right size, running downhill to an approved location, never capped or threaded at the end.
  • Thermal expansion control — an expansion tank on closed systems, which is most Bonney Lake homes with a backflow device or PRV at the meter.
  • Venting on gas units — diameter, rise, clearances, draft; sealed venting and condensate drain on power-vent and condensing units.
  • Gas connection — sediment trap, approved flex connector, shut-off valve, leak test.
  • Drip pan and drain where a leak would damage the house.
  • Combustion air and clearances for gas units in closets and small rooms.
  • Dielectric or brass transitions between copper and the steel tank.

Those are the items we see missing on bargain installs around the Plateau, and the items that cause the water-damage and carbon-monoxide calls. The permit fee buys a second set of trained eyes on all of them.

Why unpermitted work costs more later

Three ways. At resale, a buyer's inspector notes a water heater with no permit on file, and the lender or buyer asks for it to be inspected or redone — on your timeline, not yours to choose. In an insurance claim after a tank fails or a vent leaks, an unpermitted install gives the adjuster a reason to look harder. And in the day-to-day, an install nobody checked is the one with the capped relief valve, the missing expansion tank and the straps that aren't anchored to anything. The permit fee in Bonney Lake and Pierce County is modest; the inspection is included in our price.

Permits and insurance, briefly

Homeowner policies don't usually ask about permits up front, but adjusters do after a loss. A permitted, inspected water heater removes an argument before it starts; an unpermitted one invites questions about who installed it and whether the venting and relief valve met code. For a gas unit in particular, the inspection's check of the vent and combustion air is the part that protects the people in the house, not just the floor under the tank.

Tankless, heat pump and relocations

A tankless conversion needs the same plumbing permit plus gas work — the upsized line and the venting — and both get inspected. A heat pump water heater needs the circuit confirmed and the condensate drain checked. Moving a water heater to a new location, or adding one for an addition or ADU, involves new supply, drain and possibly gas and venting, and is a larger permit. We quote the permit and inspection on every water heater estimate so there's nothing to add later.

Other plumbing work that needs a permit here

Water heaters are the most common, but they're not alone. In Bonney Lake and Pierce County a permit is required for repipes, sewer and water service line repair or replacement, backflow assembly installation, moving or adding fixtures (a new bathroom, a relocated kitchen sink, a utility sink in the garage), gas line work, and most remodel rough-in. Straight replacements of a faucet, toilet or disposal in the same location generally don't. When in doubt, the jurisdiction's permit desk will tell you in a two-minute call — and so will we.

What to keep after the inspection

Keep the permit number, the inspection final, the installer's invoice with the model and serial number, and the warranty registration. Photograph the rating plate. Put the folder with the house documents, because the next owner's inspector will ask, and because a manufacturer's warranty claim on a tank that fails early is far easier with the install date and the permit in hand.

How long the inspection takes, and what if it fails

The inspector's visit is typically fifteen to twenty minutes, scheduled within the week in Bonney Lake and Pierce County, and the heater is in service the whole time. A failed inspection is uncommon on our work, but when a correction is called for — a strap re-anchored, a discharge line re-routed — we make it at no charge and request the re-inspection ourselves. You never deal with the permit office directly unless you want to. The final is emailed to you and kept in our records; if you lose it, call and we'll send another copy.
