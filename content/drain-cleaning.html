Is it one drain, or all of them?

The single most useful thing you can tell a plumber about a clog is how many drains are affected. One slow sink is a local problem in that fixture's trap or branch line. Two or more slow drains, water coming up in a tub when the toilet flushes, or gurgling from a drain you aren't using mean the blockage is in a shared line — often the main. The checker below sorts it out and tells you what to expect.

Where's the clog?

How we clear each kind of drain

  • Kitchen sinks. Grease, coffee grounds and food solids build a plug in the trap or in the branch line behind the wall — and in Bonney Lake's crawl-space homes, that branch runs through unheated space where grease sets hard. We cable from the cleanout or the trap with a grease-cutting head, run hot water to confirm flow, and check the disposal's drain connection while we're there.
  • Bathroom sinks. Hair wrapped around the pop-up stopper and soap scum in the trap. Often a hand-clear and a trap clean; a cable if it's deeper.
  • Tubs and showers. Hair and soap at the strainer and in the trap. We go through the overflow on tubs so the drain shoe isn't damaged, and through the strainer on showers.
  • Laundry standpipes. Lint and detergent sludge. A standpipe that's too short or a line that's undersized for a modern high-efficiency washer's pump is a plumbing fix, not a cleaning; we'll say which.
  • Floor drains. Garage and utility-room floor drains dry out, lose their trap seal and collect debris. We clear and refill them, and add a trap primer if they keep drying.
  • Toilets. Augered through the bowl. Repeated toilet clogs with everything else fine are usually an object in the trapway.
  • Main sewer line. Cabled from the outdoor or basement cleanout with a root-cutting head, then camera-inspected. Full detail on our drain and sewer line page.

Why drains clog on the Plateau

Bonney Lake's housing stock is mostly crawl-space construction, and drain lines that run through a cold crawl space in January do something lines in a warm slab don't: they chill whatever flows through them. Grease that left the kitchen sink as liquid sets a few feet down the line. Add Plateau water's mineral content, which leaves scale on pipe walls that gives grease and hair something to grip, and you get kitchen and laundry lines that narrow over years until a normal day's use tips them over. Older homes around Lake Tapps and the original city lots with galvanized or cast-iron drains have rough interiors that catch debris far more than smooth PVC.

That's the background to our honest answer on recurring clogs: we're glad to clear a drain every year, but if it's the same drain, a camera look costs less than the third cleaning and tells you whether the pipe has a belly, a bad joint or roots — see drain and sewer line repair.

What not to do before we arrive

  • Don't pour chemical drain cleaner. It rarely works on a real clog, it damages older pipe and PVC solvent joints, and it burns whoever opens the trap next — including us. If you already have, tell us so we can protect ourselves.
  • Don't run the dishwasher or washer into a slow line; it will overflow.
  • Do try a plunger on a sink or tub with the overflow covered, and a hand auger if you have one. Both are safe and solve a surprising share of clogs.
  • Do note the pattern: which drains, since when, and whether anything else gurgles. It's half the diagnosis.

Prevention that actually works

Hair screens in tubs and showers. No grease, fats or coffee grounds down the kitchen sink, and a hot-water flush after washing up. Lint traps on laundry hoses. Running a gallon of hot water through rarely used drains monthly so traps don't dry. And for homes with big trees over the sewer line, a yearly cabling of the main on your schedule rather than a backup on a holiday. We offer drain maintenance as part of our yearly plumbing inspection for Bonney Lake homes and small commercial plumbing customers — a restaurant's grease line, in particular, needs a plan.

Grease and the City of Bonney Lake
Grease that leaves your house doesn't disappear; it reaches the City's sewer mains or your septic tank. Keeping it out of the drain protects your line first and the system second — and if you're on septic, it extends the time between pump-outs.
