Is your sewer line at risk? A quick assessment

Sewer lines fail for predictable reasons: age, material, trees and soil. The Bonney Lake Plateau has all four working against older lines — mature firs and cedars, glacial till that shifts with saturation, and housing stock from every decade since the 1950s. Answer the questions below for a sense of where your line stands and whether a camera inspection should come before the next cleaning.

Sewer line risk check

What the camera finds under Bonney Lake

  • Root intrusion. The Plateau's big conifers send roots toward the moisture at every joint in an old clay, concrete or cast-iron line. A cable cuts them back; they return. A lined or replaced section ends it.
  • Bellies (sags). Glacial till settles unevenly, and a section of pipe drops, holding water and solids that build into a clog. Lining doesn't fix a belly; the section has to be re-laid at grade.
  • Offset and broken joints. Ground movement shifts one pipe section against the next, leaving a lip that catches paper and a gap that lets soil in.
  • Orangeburg pipe. Tar-impregnated fiber pipe used into the 1970s. It goes oval, blisters and collapses. Any Orangeburg found on camera is a replacement, not a repair.
  • Scale and grease. Cast-iron interiors rough with scale, or kitchen lines narrowed by grease, where hydro jetting restores the full bore without digging.
  • Collapse. The end state of any of the above — and a true emergency when it happens.

Repair options, chosen from the video

Spot repair

One bad joint or a short broken section, located from the surface, dug in one place and replaced with PVC and proper transition couplings. The least invasive fix when the rest of the line is sound.

Pipe lining (cured-in-place)

A resin-saturated liner is pulled through the existing pipe and cured into a new, jointless pipe inside the old one. No trench. It stops roots and seals cracks across a long run; it needs the host pipe to be round and mostly intact, and it can't fix a belly.

Pipe bursting

A new HDPE pipe is pulled through the old line while a bursting head breaks the old pipe outward. Two small pits instead of a trench. The answer for collapsed Orangeburg or badly broken clay where lining won't hold.

Open-trench replacement

Sometimes still the right choice: bellies that need re-grading, lines under shallow cover, or short runs where trenching is quicker than mobilizing trenchless equipment. We bed new PVC in gravel at the correct slope, add a cleanout at the house and at the property line, and compact the backfill so the driveway and lawn don't settle.

What's involved on the day

We call for utility locates, pull the permit with the City of Bonney Lake or Pierce County, and confirm where your lateral connects. On repair day we clear the line if needed, confirm the location with the camera, excavate or set up the trenchless rig, make the repair, camera it again to prove it, and hold for inspection before backfill. For City-sewer homes we restore service the same day; for septic homes we coordinate with your septic contractor if the tank or drainfield needs work too. We also add cleanouts where a line has none — a house with no accessible cleanout turns every future clog into a bigger job than it should be.
