Where your shut-off valves are in a Bonney Lake home

Every Bonney Lake homeowner should know two valves: the main shut-off and the water heater supply valve. In most homes on the Plateau the main valve is where the service line enters — on a garage wall, in the crawl space near the front of the house, or in a utility closet. If you can't find it, the City of Bonney Lake Water Department's meter box is at the edge of your property, usually near the street, and the valve on the house side of the meter can be closed with a meter key or a crescent wrench. Newer Tehaleh and Prairie Ridge homes typically have a clearly labeled main valve and a pressure-reducing valve together in the garage.

Emergency plumbing problems we handle around the clock

Our emergency plumbing service covers the problems that can't wait, and the crew that answers is the same crew that does our scheduled work — licensed Washington plumbers, not a subcontractor pulled off a list.

  • Burst and leaking pipes. Copper, PEX, galvanized or CPVC. We isolate, repair or replace the section, and check the rest of the run for the same failure. Older homes around the original Bonney Lake lots and Lake Tapps often have galvanized lines that fail in more than one place.
  • Water heater failures. No hot water, a tank leaking from the bottom, a relief valve that won't stop dripping, a pilot that won't stay lit. Same-day water heater replacement in most cases.
  • Sewer backups and main-line clogs. Sewage in a tub or floor drain means the main line is blocked. We clear it, camera it, and tell you whether drain line repair is needed.
  • Frozen pipes. The Plateau gets a handful of hard freezes most winters. Uninsulated crawl-space lines and hose bibs are the usual casualties.
  • Overflowing toilets and failed shut-off valves. A stuck fill valve or a corroded angle stop that won't close.
  • Slab and crawl-space leaks. Unexplained wet spots, a water meter that spins with everything off, or a sudden pressure drop.
  • Gas water heater and gas line issues. After the utility clears a leak, we inspect, repair and relight.

Burst pipes, backups and water heater failures almost always have a cause worth fixing — high pressure, roots, a missing expansion tank, an uninsulated line. We note it on your invoice and quote the fix separately. You decide whether to schedule it.

Preventing the most common Bonney Lake plumbing emergencies

A few inexpensive habits head off most of the midnight calls we get on the Plateau:

  • Before the first freeze — disconnect hoses, install frost-free hose bibs, insulate crawl-space supply lines, and leave the garage door closed on nights below freezing if the water heater lives there.
  • Every spring — flush the water heater to remove sediment, test the relief valve, and check the expansion tank's air charge.
  • Every year or two — have a camera run down the main sewer line if your lot has mature trees; a root cutting on your schedule beats a backup on a holiday weekend.
  • Any time — if your water pressure feels high (banging pipes, spraying faucets), have it measured. The fix is a pressure-reducing valve, and it protects every fixture in the house.
