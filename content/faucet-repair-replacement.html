What a drip actually costs

A faucet dripping once a second looks harmless and wastes a surprising amount of water — water the City of Bonney Lake bills you for, and if it's the hot side, water your water heater paid to heat. Use the calculator to see your number, then read on for what's probably wrong and how we fix it.

Repairs: cartridges, seats and the parts that wear

Modern faucets control water with a cartridge — ceramic disc, ball or sleeve — and older ones with a compression stem and rubber washer. Both wear, and both are replaceable on quality faucets. Here's how the symptom maps to the part:

  • Drip from the spout — worn cartridge or washer, or a scaled valve seat. We replace the cartridge with the manufacturer's part and dress or replace the seat.
  • Leak from the base of the handle or spout — O-rings or the cartridge's outer seals. A quick repair.
  • Low flow at one faucet — the aerator or inlet screens clogged with scale. Bonney Lake's groundwater does this to every faucet eventually; cleaning or replacing the aerator restores it.
  • Handle stiff, loose or spinning — a worn cartridge or a stripped handle adapter.
  • Pull-down sprayer won't retract or leaks — the hose or the weight; both replaceable.
  • Shower goes hot-cold-hot — a failing pressure-balance cartridge in the shower valve. Replaced without opening the wall on most brands.

We carry cartridges for the major brands on the truck. If the faucet needs an obscure part, we'll tell you whether waiting for it beats replacing the faucet.

Faucet replacement, done so it stays dry

A replacement is more than threading a new faucet through the sink. We remove the old unit (which in a kitchen often means fighting corroded mounting nuts — we have the tools), clean the deck, and check the sink's hole spacing against the new faucet so a deck plate isn't needed where you didn't want one. The new faucet is set straight, sealed at the deck, and connected with new braided stainless supply lines. If the shut-off valves under the sink are the old multi-turn type that no longer close, we replace them with quarter-turn stops; a faucet with no working shut-off is a house-main emergency waiting to happen. Then everything runs hot and cold for several minutes while we check each connection with a dry paper towel.

In bathrooms we handle widespread, centerset and single-hole lavatory faucets, and we can swap a centerset for a single-hole with a matching deck plate. For tubs and showers, trim-only replacement keeps the valve in the wall when it's sound; full valve replacement is covered on our tub and shower page.

Choosing a faucet that lasts on Plateau water

Hard water is tough on cheap faucets. We recommend ceramic-disc cartridges, solid brass bodies, and finishes with a manufacturer's lifetime warranty — the difference in cost is small and the difference in service life is years. Pull-down kitchen faucets with a magnetic dock and a braided hose hold up well; spray heads with rubber nozzles let you rub scale off. If your water is hard enough to spot everything, a softener is the long-term fix for every faucet in the house.

Touchless and filtered faucets
Touch and motion-activated kitchen faucets need a battery or an outlet under the sink; filtered-water faucets need an under-sink filter plumbed in. We install both and will tell you what each needs before you buy.

Before we arrive

Empty the cabinet under the sink and note the faucet's brand if it's visible on the spout or handle — it tells us which cartridge to bring. If you're replacing, count the holes in the sink deck (one, three or four) and measure the spacing between the outer ones; the new faucet must match, or come with a deck plate that covers the extras. Shut-off valves that won't turn should be left alone; forcing a corroded stop is how a drip becomes a flood, and replacing it is part of the job. Bring the old cartridge if you've already pulled it; matching by eye beats guessing from a brand name.
