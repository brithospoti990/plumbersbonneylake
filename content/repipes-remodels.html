What pipe is in your walls?

Bonney Lake's housing stock spans seventy years, and the supply pipe inside the walls changed every couple of decades. Knowing which you have tells you whether a repipe is a someday project or an overdue one. Find an exposed section — under a sink, at the water heater, in the crawl space — and run through the identifier below.

Pipe material identifier

Whole-house repipes

A repipe replaces every hot and cold supply line from the main shut-off to each fixture. In the homes we see around Lake Tapps and the original Bonney Lake lots that usually means removing galvanized steel; in the 1980s and early-90s subdivisions it often means polybutylene — the gray plastic pipe that fails without warning and that many insurers won't cover. Either way the process is the same:

  • Assessment and plan. We map every fixture, find the routes with the least drywall impact (crawl space, attic, closets), and decide PEX vs. copper for each run.
  • Day one. Water off, new lines run alongside the old ones where possible, new shut-offs at every fixture. Water is back on by evening on the new system wherever it's connected.
  • Days two to three. Remaining fixtures, the water heater connections, hose bibs, and a new main shut-off and pressure-reducing valve if the house needs one.
  • Inspection. The City of Bonney Lake or Pierce County inspects the rough-in before walls close.
  • Drywall and finish. Coordinated with a drywall contractor; we size openings to standard patch sizes to keep this quick.

The payoff is immediate: full pressure at every tap, clear water, individual shut-offs so one repair never means shutting the house down, and no more guessing where the next leak will be. On well properties we also add the sediment protection a new system deserves.

Remodel plumbing: rough-in done right

A kitchen or bathroom remodel is the one time the walls and floor are open. That's when plumbing should be moved, upsized and corrected — not after the tile is set. We work with homeowners directly and with general contractors across the Plateau on:

  • Kitchens — relocating the sink to an island (which means a drain and a vent under the floor, not just supply), adding a pot filler, a second sink, an instant hot, a dishwasher where there wasn't one, and a properly sized fridge line.
  • Bathrooms — tub-to-shower conversions, double vanities, moving the toilet, freestanding tub fillers, thermostatic shower valves, body sprays, and the 2-inch drains and proper venting that a new layout requires.
  • Additions and ADUs — new bathrooms and kitchens tied into the existing system, sized so the water heater and the main line can serve them, with a separate shut-off.
  • Laundry rooms — relocated washer boxes, standpipes that meet code, a utility sink, and a floor drain.

Every remodel rough-in gets a pressure test before inspection, and we photograph the layout inside the walls before they close so the next person — maybe us, maybe you — knows exactly where everything runs.

Why permits and inspections protect you

Repipes and remodel plumbing are permitted work in Bonney Lake and Pierce County for a reason: this is the plumbing nobody will see again for decades. An inspector confirms the venting, the slope, the pipe sizing and the pressure test before the walls close. The permit record also matters when you sell — buyers' inspectors and lenders ask about polybutylene and unpermitted work, and a documented repipe answers both questions. We pull every permit in our name, schedule the inspections, and hand you the final.

Have the main shut-off location and, if you can, a photo of the exposed pipe ready when you call; it lets us bring the right fittings and quote more accurately on the first visit.

Partial repipes
Not every house needs everything replaced at once. A bathroom's worth of galvanized, the hot side only, or the run that keeps leaking can be replaced on its own, with the rest planned for later. We'll show you where the money does the most good.
