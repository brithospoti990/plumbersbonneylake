The honest case for tankless on the Plateau

A tankless water heater heats water only as it flows, so there's no 50-gallon tank losing heat all day and no running out when the third shower starts. The units last longer than tanks — twenty years is realistic with annual descaling — and they hang on a wall. That's the upside, and it's real. The downside is that a tankless install is a bigger job than swapping a tank: the gas line usually needs upsizing, the venting is specific, and the unit has to be sized for the coldest water of the year, not the average. Companies that skip those steps are why some Bonney Lake homeowners say tankless "doesn't work." Sized and plumbed correctly, it works very well.

Sizing for Western Washington, not the brochure

Every tankless heater has a maximum output in BTUs. How many gallons per minute that becomes depends entirely on how much it has to raise the water temperature. In Bonney Lake, water entering the house from the City's spring and well supply, or from Tacoma Water in Tehaleh, drops into the 40s°F in winter. To deliver a 120°F shower that's a 75°F rise — roughly double the rise the brochure number assumes. A unit that looks generous on paper can run two showers in July and struggle with one shower and a dishwasher in January.

We size to the winter case and your real simultaneous use. For most three- and four-bedroom homes on the Plateau that means a condensing unit in the 180,000–199,000 BTU class. For large homes, multi-head showers or a soaking tub, we look at two units in parallel or a tankless feeding a small buffer tank. For a cabin or an accessory dwelling, a smaller unit is plenty. Oversizing a little costs almost nothing in operation because the unit modulates; undersizing costs you every cold morning.

Gas line, venting and the parts of the job people underestimate

A tank water heater sips gas; a tankless unit gulps it for a few minutes at a time. The half-inch gas line that fed your old 40-gallon tank almost never carries enough for a 199,000 BTU unit, especially when the furnace and range are also running. We measure the run from the meter, calculate the load for every appliance on the line, and upsize to three-quarter inch or larger where needed. Puget Sound Energy may need to confirm the meter capacity on larger homes; we coordinate that.

Venting is just as specific. Condensing units vent through PVC or polypropylene with exact length and termination rules, and produce acidic condensate that must drain to an approved location — often through a neutralizer. Non-condensing units need stainless category III vent. Both need combustion air. We route venting to meet clearance rules from windows, doors and the gas meter, and we don't terminate it where it'll ice up a walkway in January.

  • Isolation valves with service ports for descaling
  • A sediment trap on the gas drop and a leak test on the whole line
  • A water pre-filter or scale inhibitor where the water test warrants it
  • A condensate neutralizer and drain on condensing units
  • Permit, gas inspection and plumbing inspection through the City of Bonney Lake or Pierce County
  • Freeze-protection power and placement — the unit's built-in heaters only work if it has power during an outage, so we talk through that

Descaling, repair and error codes

Bonney Lake's moderately hard water leaves scale inside a tankless heat exchanger just as it does inside a tank, and the exchanger's narrow passages tolerate far less of it. The symptoms are a unit that cycles, fluctuating temperature, lower flow and eventually an error code. An annual descaling flush — circulating a mild acid through the isolation valves for about forty-five minutes — prevents all of it. We offer it as a yearly service, and we'll show you how to do it yourself if you'd rather.

Beyond scale, the common repairs we see on the Plateau are ignition failures from a dirty burner or flame rod, a clogged inlet filter after work on the City main, flow sensors fouled by sediment, venting problems after a remodel, and freeze damage on units without power during a winter outage. Most are same-visit fixes. We service all the major brands and carry the common sensors and filters.

Pair it with recirculation
Tankless units need a minimum flow to fire, so the wait for hot water at a distant tap can feel longer than with a tank. A dedicated recirculation loop or a unit with a built-in recirc mode solves it. We design the two together.

Switching from a tank: what to expect

A tank-to-tankless conversion in Bonney Lake typically takes most of a day: remove and haul the old tank, run the new gas line, mount the unit, install venting and condensate drain, connect water with isolation valves, test, and walk you through the controls.
