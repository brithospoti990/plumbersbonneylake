Toilet repairs we do every week

  • Fill valves and flappers — the running-toilet fix. We use quality replacements sized to the toilet, not universal parts that almost fit.
  • Flush valves and tank-to-bowl gaskets — for leaks between the tank and bowl, and for toilets that won't stop hissing.
  • Supply lines and shut-off valves — braided stainless lines and quarter-turn stops. A stop that won't close is a problem in the making.
  • Wax seals and flanges — for rocking toilets and water at the base. We check the flange height against the finished floor; many Bonney Lake bathrooms that got new tile ended up with a flange below the floor and a seal that can't hold.
  • Clogs — augered, and camera-inspected if they recur.
  • Handles, levers, seats and tank bolts — the small irritations, fixed while we're there.

Toilet replacement: what's involved

A replacement is a half-day job at most, and it's a good moment to fix anything under the toilet that's been hidden for years. We shut the supply, drain and remove the old unit, scrape the flange and inspect it — cracked cast-iron and broken PVC flanges are common in Lake Tapps and original Bonney Lake homes — and check the subfloor for softness. A flange below the finished floor gets a spacer or a replacement so the seal can actually compress. Then the new toilet is set on a fresh seal, bolted without over-tightening (porcelain cracks), leveled with shims that get trimmed flush, connected with a new braided supply line and, if needed, a new shut-off valve. Caulk around the base with a gap at the back so a future leak shows itself instead of hiding.

On choosing the toilet: elongated bowls and comfort-height rims are the standard now for good reason, and modern 1.28-gallon units from major brands flush better than the 3.5-gallon toilets they replace. If anyone in the house has mobility concerns, see our accessible bathroom work for grab bars and higher seats.

Hard water and toilets on the Plateau

Bonney Lake's groundwater leaves scale in two places inside a toilet: the rim jets, which slowly close up and weaken the flush, and the fill valve screen, which slows refills. Both are maintenance items we handle on a repair call. A softener stops it at the source. Iron on well water leaves the orange streaks in the bowl that no cleaner shifts; that's a filtration conversation, not a toilet problem.

The water bill test
A toilet with a leaking flapper can run a gallon every few minutes around the clock. If your City of Bonney Lake water bill jumped and nothing else changed, the toilets are the first place to look. The food-coloring test takes ten minutes.

Before we arrive

If the toilet is leaking at the base or rocking, stop using it and close the supply valve behind it. If it's running, there's no harm in waiting — but a few drops of food coloring in the tank before we get there tells both of us whether the flapper is the culprit. For a replacement, measure from the wall to the floor bolts (usually 12 inches) and tell us whether you'd prefer elongated or round, standard or comfort height; we'll bring a unit that fits. Clear the floor around the toilet and the path to the door, since the old one leaves full of water. If you're unsure what you have, a photo of the toilet and the label inside the tank lid is all we need to bring the right parts and a matching replacement if it comes to that.
