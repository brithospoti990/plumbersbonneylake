Match the filter to the problem

Walk into a big-box store in Bonney Lake and you'll find a wall of filters that all claim to fix everything. In practice each filter type solves one or two problems well and does nothing for the others. A carbon block makes chlorinated City water taste better and does nothing for iron. A sediment spin-down protects a well pump's downstream plumbing and does nothing for taste. An iron filter fixes orange staining and does nothing for hardness. Picking by symptom gets you most of the way; a quick test at your tap finishes the job. Use the selector below, then call us to confirm.

City water vs. well water on the Plateau

Where your water comes from decides what you're filtering. Most of Bonney Lake is on the City's spring-and-well supply, which is chlorinated for distribution and carries the mineral content of Plateau groundwater. Tehaleh and some county parcels are on Tacoma Water. Buckley, South Prairie, the rural stretches toward Enumclaw and the Carbon River valley around Wilkeson and Carbonado include many private wells.

  • City of Bonney Lake water — the typical complaints are chlorine taste and smell, a light hardness film, and occasional sediment after main work. A whole-house carbon filter handles the chlorine; a sediment cartridge ahead of it handles the grit; a softener handles the film.
  • Tacoma Water (Tehaleh) — softer surface water with a chlorine residual. Carbon filtration and point-of-use drinking-water polishing are usually all that's wanted.
  • Private wells — anything goes: iron, manganese, sediment, hardness, sulfur odor, and occasionally bacteria. Start with a certified lab panel, then build the treatment train from the results. Sediment protection comes first in almost every case because it protects everything downstream, including the well pump's pressure switch and the water heater.

Whole-house filtration: what we install

Point-of-entry systems go at the main after the shut-off and pressure-reducing valve, so every faucet, shower and appliance gets treated water. The most common configurations we install around Bonney Lake:

  • Sediment + carbon cartridge system — two large housings with a bypass. The workhorse for City water homes that want chlorine out and grit gone. Cartridge changes take minutes.
  • Backwashing carbon tank — a media tank that cleans itself, for larger households or anyone who doesn't want to think about cartridges. Catalytic carbon when chloramine is present.
  • Air-injection iron and sulfur filter — for wells with dissolved iron, manganese or rotten-egg odor. No chemicals; the system oxidizes and backwashes on a schedule.
  • Spin-down sediment filter — a clear-bowl screen with a flush valve, perfect ahead of a well pressure tank or as the first stage on a dirty City line.
  • UV disinfection — for wells where a lab test finds coliform bacteria, installed last in the train after sediment so the lamp can do its job.

Every whole-house installation includes a bypass loop, unions for easy service, pressure gauges where they help you see when a change is due, and the drain connection with an air gap that Washington's plumbing code requires for backwashing units.

Under-sink and drinking-water systems

If the only thing you want is better-tasting water for drinking, coffee and cooking, a point-of-use system is the efficient choice. We install carbon-block units with a dedicated faucet, and reverse osmosis systems with a storage tank for households that want the most thorough treatment at one tap. Reverse osmosis also removes the dissolved minerals that make ice cloudy, so running a line from the RO to the refrigerator's ice maker is a popular add-on. Every under-sink install gets a proper drain saddle or air-gap faucet and a check that your cabinet has room for the tank and filter changes.

Testing: what we check and when to use a lab

On the first visit we test hardness, iron, pH, chlorine and total dissolved solids at your tap. That's enough to design most City-water systems. For wells, or when a symptom doesn't match the quick test, we recommend a certified laboratory panel — bacteria, nitrate, arsenic, manganese and the rest — and we help you read the report. Pierce County and the Washington Department of Health both publish guidance for private well owners, and a lab panel is the only honest way to know what a well needs. We don't fabricate problems to sell equipment; if the water is fine, we'll tell you that too.

Plumbing-first filtration
Filters are only as good as the plumbing around them. A housing that's cross-threaded, a drain line without an air gap or a bypass that leaks will undo the best media on the market. That's why a licensed plumber — not a water-store salesperson — should install your system.

Maintenance and service

Cartridge systems need changes on a schedule that depends on your water and your household; we label the housing with the date and expected interval. Backwashing tanks need a yearly check of the valve and media. Reverse osmosis membranes last several years with pre-filter changes on time.
