Plumbing in Lakeland Hills and east Auburn

Lakeland Hills straddles the King–Pierce county line on the north shore of Lake Tapps, and it was built almost entirely from the mid-1990s through the 2010s: two-story homes on compact lots, PEX or copper supply, closed systems with pressure-reducing valves, gas water heaters in garages, and primary bathrooms at the far end of long hot-water runs. The plumbing is modern and predictable — and the first phases are now twenty-five years old, which means water heaters, disposals, fill valves and faucet cartridges are failing in the same houses within a few seasons of each other. East Auburn's older neighborhoods down the hill, and the valley neighborhoods around downtown and the Green River, add 1950s–80s housing with copper, cast iron and the occasional galvanized surprise.

From 198th Ave E we reach Lakeland Hills in about fifteen minutes by Sumner-Tapps Highway and Lake Tapps Parkway. Auburn's valley is a bit farther but still on our route.

Water in Lakeland Hills and Auburn

Lakeland Hills is served by the City of Auburn's water utility on the King County side and by other purveyors on parts of the Pierce County side; your bill tells you which. Auburn's supply comes largely from Coal Creek Springs and City wells, with regional interties — groundwater that runs moderately hard, like the rest of the Plateau, with a chlorine residual. The practical results are scale on fixtures and water heaters, aerators that clog, and a tankless heat exchanger that needs an annual descale. A tap test decides whether a softener is worth it; a carbon filter handles chlorine taste. Because Lakeland Hills sits high on the ridge, distribution pressure varies between phases, and the pressure-reducing valve at each home does real work — a failed PRV is the first thing we check when relief valves drip or faucets spit.

What fails in a Lakeland Hills home

The pattern is close to Tehaleh's, a decade older. Water heaters from the 1990s and 2000s builds are at or past their life; many homeowners replace with a tankless unit since the gas line is already there. The wait for hot water at the far bathroom is a daily irritation that a recirculation system fixes — some phases have a builder's return line already roughed in. Expansion tanks are frequently missing or waterlogged. Builder-grade toilets and faucets are being upgraded house by house. Down the hill in older Auburn, the calls shift to cast-iron drains, sewer laterals under mature trees, and galvanized supply in pre-1970 homes.

A year of plumbing in Auburn

Late fall. The first hard freeze is when we get the calls that could have been avoided: disconnect hoses, drain outbuilding lines, and make sure exposed supply runs are insulated. Auburn homes with ridge-top till above lake tapps; valley floor below should treat this as a yearly appointment, not a one-time fix.

Winter. Burst pipes, frozen hose bibs and water heaters that quit on the coldest morning. We dispatch emergencies from Bonney Lake — about 15 minutes to lakeland hills via sumner-tapps hwy — ahead of all scheduled work, and we walk you through the shut-off on the phone while the truck is moving.

Spring. The leaks winter caused show up when outdoor faucets are first used: a split frost-free stem leaking inside the wall, a crawl-space line weeping at a fitting. It is also sewer season — saturated ground and active roots mean the laterals that are going to back up this year usually do it between March and May. Water heaters from the 1990s–2000s and older auburn sewer laterals are the two calls we schedule most in Auburn at this time of year.

Summer. Irrigation goes on, which means backflow assembly tests are due and the water heater finally gets the flush it needed. It is the best season for planned work — repipes, remodel rough-ins, water heater replacements before the fall rush — because walls dry fast and the crawl space is at its driest.

Permits, inspections and who owns what

Lakeland Hills straddles the county line: King County addresses permit through the City of Auburn, Pierce County addresses through Pierce County or the City, depending on annexation. We pull the permit in our name, meet the inspector, and hand you the final — the paperwork matters when you sell. On the utility side, the water purveyor owns the main and the meter; everything from the meter into the house is yours, and that is what we repair. The same split applies to sewer: the lateral from the house to the main is the homeowner's, and we confirm the exact connection point with the utility before quoting a line repair.

Questions from Auburn homeowners

Is Lakeland Hills closer to you than to Auburn plumbers?

Usually, yes — it sits on the Plateau above Lake Tapps, about fifteen minutes from our office, which is why we treat it as a home neighborhood.

Who supplies water in Lakeland Hills?

The City of Auburn on the King County side and other purveyors on parts of the Pierce County side. Your bill tells you which; either way it's moderately hard Plateau groundwater.

My relief valve drips and the faucets spit. What's wrong?

A closed system with a missing or failed expansion tank, often with a tired pressure-reducing valve. We test pressure, replace what's needed and set the tank's air charge correctly.

Should I replace my Lakeland Hills water heater with tankless?

If the gas line and venting support it and you want endless hot water, it's a good fit. We size for winter and quote both options.
