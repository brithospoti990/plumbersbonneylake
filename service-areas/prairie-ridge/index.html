Plumbing in Prairie Ridge

Prairie Ridge was built out mostly in the 1970s and 80s, with later infill, as a large residential neighborhood between Bonney Lake and the ridge where Tehaleh now sits. The homes are split-levels, ramblers and two-stories on wooded lots — many with big firs and cedars that were saplings when the houses went in and now tower over the sewer lines. The plumbing of that era is copper supply (with some galvanized in the earliest homes and polybutylene in a few late-80s builds), cast-iron or early ABS drains, and water heaters that have been replaced at least once. It's a neighborhood in its second or third cycle of plumbing replacement, and that's most of what we do here.

We're five minutes away on 198th Ave E, which makes Prairie Ridge the easiest same-day call we have.

What should a Prairie Ridge homeowner check first?

Prairie Ridge water

Prairie Ridge is served through the City of Bonney Lake's water system, which includes a wholesale connection to Tacoma Water on Prairie Ridge Drive — so depending on the season and the system's operations, homes here may get a blend of the City's spring and well water and Tacoma's softer supply. In practice it reads as moderately hard, chlorinated groundwater most of the year, with scale on water heaters and fixtures the common complaint and a softener the common answer after a tap test. Many Prairie Ridge meters have backflow devices installed under the City's cross-connection program, which makes the house a closed system — an expansion tank at the water heater is required and often missing. Irrigation systems need an annual backflow test.

Second-cycle plumbing on wooded lots

The 1970s–80s homes in Prairie Ridge have reached the age where original cast iron and ABS drains, galvanized stubs at hose bibs, and the first-generation copper fittings all show wear. The big trees are the neighborhood's signature and its plumbing problem: roots in sewer laterals are the most common recurring call, and a camera inspection before the next cleaning is nearly always worth it. Inside, water heaters installed in the 2000s and 2010s are now due again; hose bibs on the shaded north sides freeze; and bathrooms original to the house are being remodeled — tub-to-shower conversions and fixture upgrades. Late-80s homes with gray polybutylene pipe are a repipe on sight.

A year of plumbing in Prairie Ridge

Late fall. The first hard freeze is when we get the calls that could have been avoided: disconnect hoses, drain outbuilding lines, and make sure exposed supply runs are insulated. Prairie Ridge homes with wooded lots, glacial till, big conifers over the sewer lines should treat this as a yearly appointment, not a one-time fix.

Winter. Burst pipes, frozen hose bibs and water heaters that quit on the coldest morning. We dispatch emergencies from Bonney Lake — about 5 minutes south of our office — ahead of all scheduled work, and we walk you through the shut-off on the phone while the truck is moving.

Spring. The leaks winter caused show up when outdoor faucets are first used: a split frost-free stem leaking inside the wall, a crawl-space line weeping at a fitting. It is also sewer season — saturated ground and active roots mean the laterals that are going to back up this year usually do it between March and May. Roots in sewer laterals and shaded hose bibs freezing are the two calls we schedule most in Prairie Ridge at this time of year.

Summer. Irrigation goes on, which means backflow assembly tests are due and the water heater finally gets the flush it needed. It is the best season for planned work — repipes, remodel rough-ins, water heater replacements before the fall rush — because walls dry fast and the crawl space is at its driest.

Permits, inspections and who owns what

Prairie Ridge is unincorporated Pierce County, so permits go through Pierce County Planning and Public Works, with the City of Bonney Lake handling water-system cross-connection requirements. We pull the permit in our name, meet the inspector, and hand you the final — the paperwork matters when you sell. On the utility side, the water purveyor owns the main and the meter; everything from the meter into the house is yours, and that is what we repair. The same split applies to sewer: the lateral from the house to the main is the homeowner's, and we confirm the exact connection point with the utility before quoting a line repair.

Questions from Prairie Ridge homeowners

Is Prairie Ridge on Bonney Lake water?

Yes — served through the City of Bonney Lake system, which has a Tacoma Water wholesale intertie on Prairie Ridge Drive. The water is moderately hard most of the year.

Why does my sewer back up every year or two?

Almost certainly roots from the big trees on Prairie Ridge lots. A camera shows which joints they're entering; lining or a spot repair stops it for good.

My home was built in 1988. Should I worry about polybutylene?

Check for gray plastic supply pipe under a sink or at the water heater. If it's there, we recommend a repipe before it fails — insurers often require it.

Why is my water heater's relief valve dripping?

Thermal expansion in a closed system. The City's backflow device at your meter means expanding water has nowhere to go without an expansion tank.
