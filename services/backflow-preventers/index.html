Backflow Preventer Installation, Repair & Testing in Bonney Lake, WA

If you have an irrigation system, a fire sprinkler, a well alongside City water, or a commercial building, the City of Bonney Lake's cross-connection program requires a backflow assembly — tested every year by a certified tester. We install, repair and test them, and file the report so you don't get the letter.

Do you need a backflow assembly — and a test?

The City of Bonney Lake Water Department runs a cross-connection control program, as every Washington water system is required to. In plain terms: anywhere your plumbing could send water back toward the public main — an irrigation system with fertilizer in the ground, a fire sprinkler with stagnant water, a well that shares the house with City water — a backflow assembly has to be installed and tested every year. The checker below tells you where you stand.

Backflow requirement check

What the City of Bonney Lake requires

Washington's Department of Health requires every public water system to protect its supply from cross-connections, and the City of Bonney Lake implements that through its cross-connection control program. In practice that means three things for a property owner:

  • An approved assembly of the type the hazard calls for, installed at the point where the hazard connects — typically right after the meter for whole-property protection, or on the branch feeding the irrigation or sprinkler system.
  • Testing after installation, repair or relocation, and annually, by a Washington Department of Health certified Backflow Assembly Tester (BAT), with the test report filed with the Water Department.
  • Repair when a test fails, followed by a re-test.

Tehaleh and other Tacoma Water customers follow Tacoma's program, which has the same requirements. We're set up for both, and we know which form goes where. If you've received a notice, the deadline is real — the utility can discontinue service for an untested assembly — so call before it passes.

Installation

A new assembly is sized to the line it protects and installed in an accessible, testable position: above ground in a lockable enclosure or an insulated box, with shut-off valves on both sides and clearance for the gauge. Reduced pressure assemblies need a drain path for their relief port, because they discharge water by design when something's wrong. We pull the plumbing permit with the City or Pierce County, install to the manufacturer's and the City's requirements, perform the initial test and file it. On irrigation systems we coordinate with your irrigation contractor so the assembly, the master valve and the controller all come together. For new wells on properties with City water, the assembly goes at the City service so the two supplies can never meet.

In Washington, replacing or installing a backflow assembly on potable water is plumbing work, and the test must be done by a DOH-certified BAT — we hold both the plumbing certification and the tester certification, which is why one visit covers the whole job.

Repair and winterizing

Assemblies fail their test for a short list of reasons: debris on a check seat, a worn disc or spring, a relief valve that won't open or won't seal. All are repairable with the manufacturer's kit, and we carry kits for the common makes. After repair the assembly is re-tested and the passing report filed. Freeze damage is the other failure we see every spring — an irrigation assembly left charged through a hard freeze cracks its body, and that's a replacement. Each fall we blow down and insulate irrigation assemblies, or install an insulated, heated enclosure for assemblies that must stay in service through winter, such as fire sprinklers.

The side effect nobody mentions
Once there's a backflow device at your meter, your house is a closed system. Hot water expanding in the water heater has nowhere to go, and the relief valve starts dripping. The fix is an expansion tank — we check for one on every backflow visit.

Annual testing made easy

We keep a list. Every assembly we install or test goes on our calendar, and you get a reminder a month before the City's deadline. On the day, we gauge the assembly, repair if needed, and file the report with the City of Bonney Lake Water Department or Tacoma Water — you get a copy for your records. For commercial plumbing customers with multiple assemblies we schedule them together. Questions Bonney Lake homeowners ask about backflow preventers

What is a backflow preventer?

A valve assembly that lets water flow only one way — into your property — so that if pressure drops in the City main, water from your irrigation, boiler, pool or well can't be siphoned back into the public drinking water supply.

Do I need one in Bonney Lake?

If you have lawn irrigation, a fire sprinkler system, a pool or spa auto-fill, a boiler, a well or an auxiliary water source on a property also connected to City water, or a commercial building, the City's cross-connection control program requires one. Many residential meters also now have one installed by the City.

How often does it need testing?

Annually, by a Washington Department of Health certified Backflow Assembly Tester, after installation, repair or relocation, with the report filed with the City of Bonney Lake Water Department. We test and file.

What happens if I ignore the test notice?

The City can shut off water service to a property with an untested or failed assembly. The notice usually gives a deadline; call us before it passes.

What kinds of backflow assemblies are there?

Double check valve assemblies (DCVA) for irrigation and low-hazard uses, reduced pressure backflow assemblies (RPBA) for high-hazard situations like wells, chemical injection and commercial uses, and pressure vacuum breakers (PVB) for some irrigation. The City specifies which your situation needs.

Why does my water heater drip since the City installed a backflow device?

A backflow device turns your home into a closed system — expanding hot water can't push back into the main. The fix is a thermal expansion tank, covered on our expansion tank page.

Can you winterize my backflow assembly?

Yes. Assemblies on irrigation lines sit outdoors and freeze in Plateau winters; we blow down and insulate them, or install them with a freeze-protected enclosure.
