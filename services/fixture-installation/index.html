Plumbing Fixture Installation in Bonney Lake, WA

New vanity, new sink, new shower head, a bidet seat, a utility sink in the garage — the fixtures that make a house yours. We install them properly: level, sealed, vented, with working shut-offs, and without surprising the next person who opens the cabinet.

Build your fixture list

Fixture jobs rarely come one at a time. A new vanity usually means a new faucet; a kitchen refresh means a sink, faucet, disposal and maybe an instant hot. Doing them in one visit saves you the trip charge and us the setup. Tick what you're planning and the tool tells you what each needs behind the wall, so there are no surprises on the day.

The fixtures we install most in Bonney Lake homes

Vanities and bathroom sinks

The most common fixture job on the Plateau. New vanities are almost never the same height or depth as the old one, so the drain trap has to be re-cut and the supplies repositioned — the difference between a clean cabinet and a flexible accordion trap that clogs. We set the vanity level, seal the countertop to the wall, install the faucet and pop-up drain, and replace the shut-off valves if they're the old type.

Kitchen sinks

Undermount sinks in stone or quartz need the right clips and a properly supported bowl; drop-ins need a clean seal. Either way the basket strainers, trap, disposal and dishwasher drain all get reconnected, and the air gap gets re-drilled if the new sink's deck layout changed. A deep farmhouse sink often means a shorter trap arm and a relocated disposal — we plan for it.

Toilets, bidets and bidet seats

Toilet replacement is covered on its own page. Bidet seats are the fastest-growing fixture request we get: a cold tee at the toilet supply with a real valve, and for heated models a GFCI outlet within reach. Stand-alone bidets need a drain and both supplies.

Shower heads, hand showers and body sprays

Swapping a shower head is easy; adding a hand shower on a slide bar needs backing in the wall for the bar, and a diverter. Body sprays and multi-head showers need a valve and supply sized for the flow, which is a shower valve job.

Utility, laundry and garage sinks

A deep sink by the washer or in the garage is one of the most useful additions to a house. It needs hot and cold supply, a drain tied into the main with a vent, and on the Plateau, routing that won't freeze in an unheated garage. This is usually permitted work.

Pot fillers and specialty fixtures

A pot filler over the range needs a cold line inside the wall to a drop-ear fitting at the right height, which is easiest during a remodel but doable afterward with a small wall opening. Bar sinks, drinking-water faucets, outdoor showers and dog-wash stations follow the same logic: supply, drain, vent, freeze protection, done once.

How we install

We start by confirming the fixture fits — rough-in, hole spacing, drain location, cabinet depth — because the wrong fixture is the most expensive thing to discover on install day. Old fixtures come out and go to recycling. Supply stubs get new quarter-turn shut-offs where the existing valves are worn; drains get a proper trap and, where the fixture is new, a vent connection that meets Washington's plumbing code. Wall-mounted items get solid backing. Everything is set level and plumb, sealed, and run under full pressure while we check each joint. When a job adds plumbing rather than replacing it, we pull the permit with the City of Bonney Lake or Pierce County and meet the inspector.

Buying fixtures online?
Send us the model numbers before you order. We'll check the rough-in and hole pattern against your sink or wall, and flag the ones that arrive without the parts you need — a fifteen-minute email that saves a week of returns.

Before we arrive

Have every fixture on site, unboxed enough to confirm nothing's cracked and all the parts are there. Clear the cabinets and the floor. For vanities, confirm the countertop and sink arrive together; for wall-mounted items, let us know if the wall has been opened recently — backing is far easier to add before the drywall closes. If any item is going somewhere it hasn't been before, send a photo of the spot and the nearest plumbing you can see; it helps us route accurately and quote once.

Questions Bonney Lake homeowners ask about fixture installation

What counts as a fixture?

Anything water comes out of or goes into: sinks, faucets, toilets, tubs, showers and their heads and valves, bidets and bidet seats, utility and bar sinks, pot fillers, outdoor showers, drinking fountains and hose bibs. If you bought it and it needs water, we install it.

Can you install fixtures I bought online?

Yes. Send us the model and we'll check it fits your rough-in, hole spacing and drain location before the visit, so there's no return shipping.

Do I need a permit to install a new sink or toilet?

Replacing a fixture in the same spot typically doesn't. Moving a fixture, adding one where there wasn't one, or changing drain or vent plumbing does, through the City of Bonney Lake or Pierce County. We'll tell you and handle it.

Can you add a bathroom or utility sink in the garage?

Yes. It needs a supply, a vented drain and often a hot line; garages on the Plateau also need freeze consideration. We plan the route and quote it in writing.

How long does a vanity install take?

A vanity swap with the drain in the same place is two to four hours including faucet, trap and supply lines. Moving the drain or supply adds time.

Do you install bidet seats?

Yes — they need a cold supply tee at the toilet and, for heated models, a GFCI outlet nearby. We install the tee with a proper valve and coordinate an electrician for the outlet if needed.

Will you remove and dispose of old fixtures?

Yes. Old vanities, sinks and toilets are hauled away as part of the job.
