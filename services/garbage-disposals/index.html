Garbage Disposal Installation, Replacement & Repair in Bonney Lake, WA

A disposal that hums, leaks under the sink or trips the breaker is a fifteen-minute diagnosis for us. We repair what's worth repairing, replace with the right horsepower for how your kitchen is used, and plumb the drain and dishwasher connection so nothing backs up.

Hum, jam, leak or silence: start here

Most disposal problems announce themselves clearly, and most have a specific fix. Before you call — or so you know what to tell us when you do — run through the troubleshooter below. The one rule: switch the disposal off at the wall, and ideally at the breaker, before putting anything near it. Never put your hand in the chamber.

Disposal troubleshooter

Repair or replace?

Disposals are inexpensive enough that the decision is mostly about age and what failed. A unit under about five years old with a jam, a loose connection or a failed flange seal is worth repairing. A unit past eight to ten years, or one leaking from the body, rattling on its bearings, or tripping its reset weekly, is done — the labor to open it up costs more than the labor to swap it. We tell you which you've got before touching it, and if it's a replacement we usually have a three-quarter-horsepower unit on the truck.

Around Bonney Lake we also see a lot of builder-grade third-horsepower units in homes from the 2000s and 2010s. They were never meant for a family that cooks every night. If yours stalls on potato peels or anything fibrous, stepping up to three-quarter or one horsepower fixes the stalling and, counterintuitively, makes the kitchen quieter.

What a proper installation includes

  • Resealed sink flange with fresh putty, so the top never leaks.
  • Correct drain geometry. The disposal's outlet must sit above the trap arm with fall toward it; many kitchens around Lake Tapps and the older Bonney Lake lots were plumbed with the trap too high, which is why they back up. We re-cut the trap if needed.
  • Dishwasher connection through the disposal's port with the knockout removed, a high loop under the counter or an air gap at the sink deck as code requires, and new clamps.
  • Electrical — a plug-in unit to a switched outlet, or hardwired through the mounting box with a proper strain relief. If there's no switch or outlet, we coordinate an electrician or install an air-switch on the counter.
  • A test with real water flow, checking every joint under the sink with a dry paper towel.
  • Walk-through: cold water on before and after, what not to put down it (fibrous peels, grease, coffee grounds, eggshells in quantity), and how to clear a jam yourself.

Disposals, septic and the Plateau's drains

A disposal adds ground food to whatever is downstream. On a City of Bonney Lake sewer connection that's no issue. On a septic system — common around Buckley, South Prairie, Wilkeson and the rural edges of Orting and Enumclaw — it adds solids to the tank, and we recommend a septic-rated unit, a little restraint with scraps, and pump-outs on a slightly shorter schedule. If your kitchen drain already runs slowly, a new disposal won't fix that and will show it faster; a quick drain cleaning on the same visit is often the right pairing.

Grease is the other Plateau-specific note. Cold winter crawl spaces chill drain lines, and grease that was liquid at the sink solidifies a few feet down the pipe. Disposals don't cause this, but they're blamed for it. Hot water and dish soap after every use, and never pouring fat down the drain, keeps the line clear.

Pair it with a new faucet or sink
If you're replacing the sink or faucet anyway, do the disposal at the same time — the flange and trap come apart once instead of twice. See faucet replacement and fixture installation.

Before we arrive

If the disposal is leaking, close the dishwasher's connection by not running it, put a bucket under the unit and clear the cabinet so we can get to the mounting ring. If it's jammed, leave it — we'd rather free it ourselves than find a bent impeller from a screwdriver. Have the model number handy if you can read it on the side of the unit; it tells us whether a direct replacement will land on your existing mounting ring, which makes the swap faster. And if you've been meaning to replace the faucet or add a filter at the same sink, say so when you call: the cabinet only needs emptying once.

Questions Bonney Lake homeowners ask about garbage disposals

My disposal hums but doesn't spin. Is it dead?

Usually not. A hum means the motor has power but the flywheel is jammed. With the power off, a hex key in the socket on the bottom frees most jams, then the red reset button restarts it. If it jams repeatedly or the hum comes with a burning smell, the bearings are going and replacement makes more sense.

Why is water leaking under my sink from the disposal?

Three common places: the sink flange where it meets the sink (putty failed), the dishwasher inlet or drain connection (loose clamp or cracked fitting), or the body itself (internal seal failed). The first two are repairs; a leaking body means a new unit.

What horsepower do I need?

Half-horsepower units handle light use in a small household. Three-quarter is the right choice for most Bonney Lake families. One horsepower makes sense for heavy cooking households, farmhouse sinks with lots of scraps, or anyone who wants it quieter — bigger units usually run quieter.

Is a disposal OK with a septic system?

Yes, with a septic-rated unit and the understanding that it adds solids to the tank, so pump-outs come a little sooner. Many properties around Buckley, South Prairie and rural Orting are on septic; we'll tell you what to expect.

Can you connect the dishwasher to the new disposal?

Yes — the dishwasher drains through a port on the disposal. We knock out the plug (the most common install mistake is forgetting to), install the hose with a high loop or an air gap as Washington code requires, and test both.

How long does replacement take?

About an hour for a like-for-like swap. Add time if the drain trap needs rework, the sink flange needs resealing, or the old unit was hardwired and the new one needs an outlet and switch.

Do I need a permit?

Not for a straight replacement. New installations that add a circuit or change drain plumbing may; we handle it.
