Ice Maker & Refrigerator Water Line Installation in Bonney Lake, WA

The fridge delivery crew won't run the water line, and the saddle valve someone installed in 1998 is a leak waiting to happen. We run a proper line from a real shut-off valve to your refrigerator — through cabinets, crawl space or wall — and it stays dry.

The small line behind the big appliance

A refrigerator water line is a quarter-inch of plumbing that most people never think about until it fails. When it fails, it fails behind the fridge or inside the sink cabinet — the two places water sits unnoticed longest. Around Bonney Lake we're called to a lot of swollen cabinet floors, warped flooring under refrigerators and, occasionally, a ceiling below a second-floor kitchen, all from a line that cost almost nothing and was installed with a saddle valve and plastic tubing. Done properly, the line is a non-event for the life of the house.

Plan your refrigerator line

What we install, and what we take out

Every refrigerator line we run starts at a real valve: a tee into the cold supply with a quarter-turn ball valve, not a self-piercing saddle clamp. From there the line is braided stainless or PEX, protected where it passes through cabinets or framing, with a recessed fridge box or a second shut-off at the appliance end so you can pull the fridge out without crawling under the sink. Connections are compression or push-fit with the correct inserts, and every one is checked under pressure before we leave.

What we take out, almost every time, is a saddle valve. They were sold in every hardware store for decades and they share one flaw: the needle that pierces the pipe leaves a pinhole that scales shut on Bonney Lake water, and the rubber seal around it weeps as it ages. Washington's plumbing code no longer allows them. If you have one under your sink — a small clamp with a wheel handle on the cold line — replacing it is the single most useful thing on this page.

Routing the line: three common Bonney Lake kitchens

  • Fridge beside the sink. The easy one: a tee under the sink, a hole through the cabinet partition, a short run along the back, and a valve box behind the fridge.
  • Fridge across the room or on an island wall. In the Plateau's many crawl-space homes we drop under the floor from the sink cabinet and come back up behind the fridge, insulating the run. In slab homes we go above the upper cabinets or through the wall.
  • Second fridge in the garage, or a wet bar. A new cold branch from the nearest line, routed to avoid exterior walls where freezing is a risk, with a drain-down valve so the line can be emptied before a hard freeze if the garage isn't heated.

Every route gets a shut-off at the fridge end and protection where the line is exposed. We don't leave tubing lying under a fridge where a wheel can pinch it.

Better ice: filters and reverse osmosis

Bonney Lake's water is chlorinated for distribution, and the City's spring and well sources carry enough dissolved mineral to make ice cloudy and leave scale in the fridge's valve over time. The fridge's built-in filter removes chlorine taste but not minerals. For clear ice and water that tastes like the filtered tap, we can feed the refrigerator line from an under-sink reverse osmosis system with a small booster if the run is long. On a private well, an inline sediment filter ahead of the fridge keeps grit out of a solenoid valve that's expensive to replace. If the house has a softener, the fridge line normally comes off the softened side.

Leak-detection add-on
A battery leak sensor behind the fridge, or an automatic shut-off valve on the line, costs little and catches the one failure this plumbing can have. We install both on request — worth it under a second-floor kitchen.

Before we arrive

Pull the fridge out a foot if you can, or we will; have the refrigerator's installation sheet handy for the inlet fitting size (most are quarter-inch compression). Clear the sink cabinet. If the run will cross a crawl space, let us know whether the access hatch is inside or outside. For a fridge with a filter that's never been changed, pick up a replacement — a new line feeding a clogged filter still gives you slow water.

Same visit, same cabinet

An ice maker line shares the under-sink space with the dishwasher supply, the disposal and any instant hot dispenser. If any of those need attention, one visit covers them. Questions Bonney Lake homeowners ask about ice maker water lines

What is a saddle valve and why do plumbers hate them?

A self-piercing clamp that bites into a copper pipe. They're banned under Washington's plumbing code because the tiny hole clogs with scale and the seal eventually weeps — often inside a cabinet where nobody looks. We replace them with a proper tee and quarter-turn valve.

How far can the line run from the sink to the fridge?

Across a kitchen, through the crawl space or basement, or up through a wall — distance isn't the issue as long as the line is protected. We've run them across islands and through garages to a second fridge.

Copper, PEX or braided — which line?

Braided stainless or PEX from a quarter-turn valve is our standard: flexible, doesn't kink, doesn't corrode. Copper is fine where it's protected. Plastic tubing from a kit is what we're usually replacing.

Should I add a filter?

Most refrigerators have a built-in filter. If you want better-tasting ice and water on Bonney Lake's chlorinated supply, or you're on a well, an inline filter or a feed from an under-sink reverse osmosis system makes a real difference. We can plumb the fridge line off an RO system.

Will the line freeze in the crawl space?

Water sitting in a line through an unheated crawl space can freeze in a hard Plateau winter. We route to avoid it where possible, insulate where it's exposed, and tell you if a different route is smarter.

How long does it take?

A line from the sink cabinet to an adjacent fridge is about an hour. Longer runs through a crawl space or wall take two to three.

Can you connect a water line for a coffee maker or a pot filler?

Yes — same job, different end fitting. Plumbed coffee machines, pot fillers over the range and wet-bar fridges all get the same proper shut-off and braided line.
