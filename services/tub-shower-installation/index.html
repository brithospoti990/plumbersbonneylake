Tub & Shower Installation and Replacement in Bonney Lake, WA

Whether it's a worn-out tub, a shower valve that's gone hot-cold-hot, or a tub you'd rather turn into a walk-in shower, the plumbing behind the tile decides whether the bathroom works for the next twenty years. We do the rough-in, the valve, the drain and the trim — and coordinate the tile and surround.

Tub, shower, or both? Find the right fit

The first decision in any tub-and-shower project isn't the brand of valve — it's what the bathroom should be. A family with young kids needs a tub. A primary bath that nobody bathes in wants a walk-in shower. A home with an aging parent might need a low-threshold shower now and a walk-in tub later. The short quiz below gives an honest starting point.

Which setup suits this bathroom?

Tub replacement

Alcove tubs are the workhorse of Bonney Lake bathrooms, and most of the ones we replace are original to the house — steel tubs with chipped enamel in the older Lake Tapps and city-lot homes, fiberglass units from the 1990s with flex and cracks. Replacement is a plumbing job wrapped in a carpentry job: the old tub comes out with the surround, the drain and overflow are rebuilt with a new waste-and-overflow assembly, the valve is replaced or re-trimmed, the new tub is set level in a bed of mortar so it doesn't flex, and the surround or tile goes in before the trim. We handle the plumbing on both ends and work with your tile setter in between, or install an acrylic surround ourselves for a one-contractor job.

Deeper soaking tubs and freestanding tubs are increasingly popular in primary baths. They change the plumbing: a larger drain and overflow, a floor-mounted or wall-mounted filler, and a check that the water heater can actually fill them.

Shower valves: the part that makes or breaks the shower

The valve in the wall controls temperature and flow, and it's what fails when a shower runs lukewarm, drips, or swings hot-cold-hot when a toilet flushes. Washington's plumbing code requires a pressure-balance or thermostatic anti-scald valve on new shower installations, and it's the first thing we recommend upgrading in any older bathroom. Options:

  • Pressure-balance valve — holds temperature steady when pressure changes elsewhere in the house. The standard for most showers.
  • Thermostatic valve — you set a temperature and it holds it, with separate volume control. The choice for multi-head showers and anyone who wants the same temperature every time.
  • Diverters and transfer valves — for hand showers, body sprays and rain heads, so each outlet can run alone or together.

Replacing a valve body means getting to it. If there's a closet or hallway on the other side of the shower wall, we open that side and the tile stays untouched. If not, a remodel cover plate lets us replace the valve through the tile with a finished look. Trim-only replacement — new handle and plate on a sound valve — is the cheapest refresh and takes an hour.

Tub-to-shower conversions

This is the project we're asked about most in primary bathrooms across Tehaleh, Sumner and Bonney Lake. The tub comes out, and the plumbing changes in three ways: the drain moves to the center of the new shower and goes from 1½-inch to 2-inch with a proper trap; a waterproof pan — a mortar bed and liner for tile, or a prefab base — replaces the tub floor; and the valve moves up to shower height with a new anti-scald cartridge. Add a bench, a niche, a hand shower on a slide bar and grab-bar backing in the walls while they're open, and the bathroom is ready for every stage of life. Low-threshold and curbless designs for accessibility are covered in more depth on our accessible bathing page.

Drains, overflows and the leak you can't see

The quiet failure in tub and shower work is the drain. A tub waste-and-overflow with a dried-out gasket leaks into the ceiling below every bath; a shower pan with a missed weep hole or no pre-slope holds water under the tile until it finds the subfloor. On every job we replace the waste-and-overflow assembly, test the drain by filling, and on tile showers we flood-test the pan before anything goes over it. Homes in the Lake Tapps area and the older parts of Bonney Lake with original cast-iron drains often need the trap and arm replaced too; we check while the floor is open.

Match the water heater to the tub
A 60-gallon soaking tub needs most of a 50-gallon water heater's contents at full temperature. If you're adding a big tub, we check the heater's capacity and recovery — and if it's marginal, a tankless unit or a larger tank goes in the plan.

Questions Bonney Lake homeowners ask about tub & shower installation

Can you convert my bathtub to a walk-in shower?

Yes. It's one of the most common bathroom projects on the Plateau. We remove the tub, rework the drain to a shower drain with a proper pan and liner or a prefab base, set the valve at shower height, and coordinate the surround or tile. It typically needs a permit.

Why does my shower go hot and cold when someone flushes?

The shower valve has no pressure balancing, or its pressure-balance cartridge has failed. Washington code requires an anti-scald valve on new shower installs; replacing the valve fixes it for good.

Can you replace the shower valve without opening the tile?

Trim and cartridges, yes. Replacing the valve body itself needs access — from the back side of the wall if there's a closet or hallway behind it, or through the tile with a remodel plate that covers the opening. We find the least invasive route.

Do you install the tile and surround?

We do the plumbing: rough-in, valve, drain, overflow and trim. For tile, pans and surrounds we work alongside your tile setter or can recommend one we've worked with. Acrylic surrounds and prefab shower bases we install ourselves.

How long does a tub replacement take?

Plumbing for a like-for-like tub swap is a day. A tub-to-shower conversion or a full tub-and-surround replacement is several days including the surround or tile, with the plumbing on the first and last days.

Is a permit required?

Replacing a tub in the same spot with the same drain usually needs a permit for the plumbing in Bonney Lake and Pierce County; conversions always do. We pull it and schedule the inspection.

What about a freestanding tub?

Freestanding tubs need the drain located precisely and a floor- or wall-mounted filler, often with a supply run under the floor. It's best planned during a remodel; we can do it in an existing bathroom with a crawl space below.
