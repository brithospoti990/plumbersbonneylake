Water Heater Installation, Replacement & Repair in Bonney Lake, WA

No hot water is the second-most-common emergency call we get on the Plateau. We stock the common tank sizes for same-day replacement, repair what's worth repairing, and install every unit to Washington code with the seismic strapping, expansion tank and venting your inspector will look for.

Repair, replace or upgrade: how we decide

A water heater call starts with three questions: how old is it, where is it leaking (if it's leaking), and what else is going on in the house's plumbing. The age is on the rating plate — the serial number encodes the manufacture date and we'll show you how to read it. A tank that's leaking from the bottom or from a rusted seam is done; no repair will hold. A unit that's leaking from a fitting on top, won't light, or runs out of hot water fast is often repairable. And a house where the relief valve drips or the pressure runs high needs the underlying problem solved or the new heater will fail early too.

We give you both numbers — repair and replacement — on a written estimate, with our honest recommendation. The calculator below gives you a head start on sizing if you're replacing.

What size water heater do I need?

Based on first-hour-rating guidelines. Gas recovers faster than electric, so it can get away with a smaller tank. We confirm on site against your space, venting and gas line.

What a code-compliant installation includes in Bonney Lake

A water heater is one of the few appliances that combines pressurized water, high temperature and — for gas units — combustion and exhaust in one steel can, which is why Washington treats replacement as permitted plumbing work. Here's what's on our checklist for every tank install on the Plateau:

  • Permit and inspection through the City of Bonney Lake or Pierce County, depending on your address.
  • Seismic strapping — two straps, upper and lower third, anchored to framing. Western Washington requires it and inspectors check it first.
  • Expansion tank sized to the heater and set to your house pressure. Most Bonney Lake homes are closed systems because of a backflow preventer or pressure-reducing valve at the meter; without an expansion tank the relief valve drips and the tank fails early. See our expansion tank page.
  • Temperature and pressure relief valve with a discharge line run to an approved location, never capped.
  • Drip pan with a drain when the heater sits where a leak would cause damage — inside the house, in a closet, or on an upper floor.
  • Venting — correct diameter, rise, clearances and draft for atmospheric gas units; sealed PVC venting for power-vent models; a condensate drain for high-efficiency units.
  • Gas connection with a sediment trap and a new flex connector, leak-tested.
  • Dielectric unions or brass nipples between copper plumbing and the steel tank to prevent galvanic corrosion.
  • A shut-off valve on the cold inlet so the heater can be isolated for service.

Water heater repair

Plenty of water heaters in Bonney Lake are retired years early because the first plumber only quoted a replacement. If your unit is under about eight years old and the tank isn't leaking, these are worth fixing:

  • No hot water (electric) — a tripped high-limit, a failed upper element or a bad thermostat. Elements on Plateau water often fail early from scale; we replace them and flush the tank.
  • No hot water (gas) — a thermocouple or flame sensor, a dirty burner, a failed gas control valve, or a vent problem that keeps the pilot from staying lit.
  • Not enough hot water — a failed lower element, a broken dip tube that mixes cold into the hot outlet, or a tank half-full of sediment.
  • Rumbling and popping — sediment on the bottom of the tank boiling water underneath it. A thorough flush fixes it if caught early.
  • Rotten-egg smell in hot water only — the anode rod reacting with the water. An aluminum-zinc or powered anode solves it.
  • Dripping relief valve — thermal expansion; an expansion tank, not a new valve.

Tank types we install

Choosing a replacement isn't just gallons. The fuel you have, the space the heater lives in, and how your household uses hot water all steer the choice.

  • Standard atmospheric gas — the most common unit in Bonney Lake garages. Reliable, fast recovery, needs a metal vent with proper draft.
  • Power-vent gas — when the heater is in a closet or interior room with no chimney; vents through PVC with a fan.
  • Standard electric — simple and compact, slower recovery. Common in Lake Tapps homes without gas service.
  • Heat pump (hybrid) electric — the efficient choice for electric homes, pulls heat from the surrounding air. Needs space and a condensate drain; Puget Sound Energy and other local utilities periodically offer incentives.
  • Tankless — endless hot water and wall-mounted; needs correct gas sizing and venting. Covered in full on our tankless water heater page.
Add-ons worth doing at the same time
A recirculation pump if the far bathroom waits minutes for hot water; a leak-detection shut-off valve if the heater is upstairs or over finished space; and a softener if scale killed the last unit.

Questions Bonney Lake homeowners ask about water heater installation

How long does a water heater last in Bonney Lake?

Tank water heaters on Plateau water typically last 8 to 12 years; hard water and a missing expansion tank shorten that, a softener and regular flushing extend it. The install date is on the rating plate — if it's past ten years and leaking, replacement is almost always the right call.

Can you replace my water heater the same day?

Usually. We keep common 40- and 50-gallon gas and electric units on the truck or in stock in Bonney Lake. Larger tanks, power-vent units and tankless models may take a day to source.

Do I need a permit to replace a water heater?

Yes. The City of Bonney Lake and Pierce County both require a plumbing permit for water heater replacement, and gas units also involve the gas connection. We pull the permit and schedule the inspection as part of the job.

Why is my water heater's relief valve dripping?

Usually thermal expansion in a closed system — common in Bonney Lake because many homes have a backflow preventer or pressure-reducing valve at the meter. The fix is an expansion tank, not a new relief valve.

Repair or replace?

Repair makes sense for a failed thermostat, element, gas valve, thermocouple or anode on a heater under about eight years old. Replace when the tank itself leaks, the unit is past ten years with a major failure, or repairs are approaching half the cost of a new unit.

Should I switch to tankless?

Tankless makes sense for households that run out of hot water, want to reclaim space, or plan to stay long enough to recover the higher install cost. It needs the right gas line and venting. See our tankless page, or ask — we install both and will recommend the one that fits.

Do you install heat pump water heaters?

Yes. Hybrid heat pump units are the most efficient electric option and qualify for utility incentives in the Puget Sound area. They need room air to work from, so garage and utility-room installs are the best fit.
